Water cycle notes, Year 8 science

- Water moves between the sea, the air and the land in a loop called the water cycle. The sun powers it.
- Evaporation: the sun heats water in seas, lakes and rivers and it turns into water vapour (a gas) that rises.
- Transpiration: plants lose water vapour through tiny holes in their leaves. Evaporation + transpiration together = evapotranspiration.
- Condensation: as water vapour rises the air gets colder, so the vapour cools and turns back into tiny liquid droplets. Lots of droplets make clouds.
- Precipitation: when droplets join and get too heavy they fall as rain, snow, sleet or hail.
- Collection: water that falls collects in oceans, lakes and rivers. Some runs over the ground (surface runoff) into rivers.
- Infiltration: some water soaks into the ground and becomes groundwater. It can stay there a long time and slowly flows back to rivers and the sea.
- About 97% of Earth's water is salty sea water. Only about 3% is fresh water and most of that is frozen in ice caps and glaciers.
- Warmer temperatures mean more evaporation.
- Trees cut down = less transpiration, so the air above can get drier. (check this w/ Mr Patel)
- The total amount of water on Earth stays about the same, it just changes state and place.
- Key words: state change, water vapour, runoff, groundwater, reservoir (a store of water like a lake or an ice cap)
