# Rome for 4 days in early June: family plan (2 adults + 9-year-old)

Plan notes: early June is warm (about 27-30°C / 80-86°F) and busy. Your kid fades after lunch, so each day has **active mornings, a long lunch, then something easy** (gelato, a park, a fountain, a slow stroll). Museums are minimal. There is one optional, kid-friendly highlight.

## Day 1: Historic center, easy start
- **Morning:** Piazza Navona, then the Pantheon (quick look inside, about 20 min). Walk to Trevi Fountain. Go early, before 9:30, to beat the crowds.
- **Lunch:** Pasta in a trattoria near the Pantheon, such as Armando al Pantheon (book ahead) or a casual spot on a side street.
- **Afternoon (low energy):** Gelato at Giolitti or Fatamorgana. Stroll to the Spanish Steps and sit for a while.
- **Dinner:** Early, around 7:30. Pizza al taglio or a casual pizzeria.

## Day 2: Ancient Rome, done lightly
- **Morning:** Colosseum exterior and Roman Forum views from the street and from Via dei Fori Imperiali. If you want to go inside, book timed tickets ahead. Kids often love the Colosseum, so this is the one "sight" worth considering.
- **Lunch:** Monti neighborhood, a pretty, walkable area with good trattorias.
- **Afternoon:** Wander Monti's lanes and Piazza della Madonna dei Monti. Optional: Villa Borghese gardens (bike or rowboat rental) if energy is left.
- **Dinner:** Cacio e pepe or carbonara in Monti.

## Day 3: Trastevere and the Gianicolo
- **Morning:** Campo de' Fiori market, then cross to the Jewish Ghetto for fried artichokes (carciofi alla giudia) and pastries at Boccione.
- **Lunch:** Long lunch in the Ghetto.
- **Afternoon:** Slow walk across the Tiber Island to Trastevere. Rest at a cafe, then wander the lanes.
- **Late afternoon:** Gianicolo Hill for sunset views. Take a taxi up if tired.
- **Dinner:** Trastevere, which is lively and good for kids. Book ahead.

## Day 4: Vatican area, flexible
- **Morning:** St. Peter's Square and Basilica. Go early, since the dress code requires covered shoulders and knees. Skip the Vatican Museums. Optionally climb the dome (about 550 steps, so check your kid's stamina).
- **Lunch:** Prati neighborhood, which has less touristy, better-value food than near the Vatican. Try Pizzarium (Bonci) for famous pizza al taglio.
- **Afternoon:** Castel Sant'Angelo and a walk along the Tiber, or a free afternoon for souvenirs and gelato.
- **Farewell dinner:** Pick a favorite from the trip, or try a Roman classic such as supplì, amatriciana or saltimbocca.

## Practical tips
- **Heat:** Start by 8:30-9:00. Rest 1-4 pm if needed. Bring water. Refill at the free *nasoni* drinking fountains.
- **Kid energy:** Build in a hotel break after lunch on any day when your kid is wiped out. Rome is walkable, but use taxis or the app FreeNow for tired legs.
- **Reservations:** Book popular dinners and any Colosseum or dome tickets in advance.
- **Dining times:** Romans eat dinner late. Many restaurants open at 7:00-7:30 pm, which suits kids better.
- **Where to stay:** Centro Storico, Monti, or Trastevere, for walkability.
- **Pickpockets:** Be careful around Termini, the Colosseum and crowded sites.

## Budget (mid-range, rough per day for 3 people)
- Food: about €100-150
- Sights and tickets: €20-60
- Local transport and taxis: €10-30
