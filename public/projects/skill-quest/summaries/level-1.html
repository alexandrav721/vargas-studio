AI, in plain words

01Meet the chef.

  • Claude is your chef.
  • It has never tasted food.
  • So once in a while, it invents a dish.
Who is it for?
What do I have?
What do I never want?
How fast / how big?
Then ask: Which facts should I double-check?

03Make it like this.

  • “Friendly but professional” means a hundred things.
  • Show the chef a dish you like.
  • Your example is your style.
What do I need? (the job)
Who is it to?
Paste one short thing I really wrote.
Say: Copy the tone and length, not the facts.
Clean out names and numbers first.

05Your own station.

  • A chat forgets.
  • A Project is a station for one job.
  • Set it up once.
One station per job (trip, garden, shop).
Instructions: who, budget, pace, never list, how answers should look.
Files: helpful ones, with booking and ID numbers taken out.
Start those chats inside the station.
Update it when plans change.

08Write your recipe card.

  • A recipe card is a skill.
  • Four parts.
  • Then taste-test it.

02Placing your order.

  • A vague order gets a vague dish.
  • Details are flavor.
  • You can send it back.
Who is it for, and one true detail?
What tone?
How long?
End with: Ask me anything you need first.
Then send it back with one change: shorter / warmer / mention ___.

04Taste before you serve.

  • The chef can be sure and wrong.
  • So taste it first.
  • Some things never go in the pot.
Taste before you serve:
1. Does it make sense?
2. Can I check it somewhere real?
3. Would I bet on it?
Ask: How sure are you, and where can I check?

06Take-home containers.

  • Food you can’t carry home is no use.
  • Name the container.
  • Say what to leave out.
For the fridge: a checklist with boxes
For a group chat: a short text under 60 words
For who-does-what: a table (who, what, when)
For a week: a day-by-day list
Always add: No intro, no closing line.

07Pantry and meal kits.

  • Your pantry is what’s always on the shelf.
  • Claude has a place for it.
  • Meal kits are ready-made skills.
